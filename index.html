Where should you begin?

You don't need expensive equipment. Start with the objects that are easiest to recognize and work your way outward.

🌙

The Moon

Earth's nearest natural neighbor changes appearance throughout the month. Its craters are surprisingly easy to see with binoculars.

⭐

Constellations

Patterns such as Orion and the Big Dipper can become familiar landmarks that help you navigate the rest of the sky.

🪐

Planets

Bright planets can stand out from the stars. With a telescope, some reveal moons, rings, or atmospheric detail.

Cosmic facts

A few reminders that make the universe feel a little stranger.

Light is a time machine.

When you look at a distant object, you see light that has traveled across space before reaching your eyes.

Our Sun is a star.

It only appears dramatically larger and brighter because Earth is much closer to it than to the other stars.

Space is enormous.

The observable universe contains an immense number of galaxies, each containing vast populations of stars.

💡 Try this tonight: Turn off nearby lights, give your eyes 15–20 minutes to adjust, and simply look upward. You may notice far more than you expect.
